About Me

I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate at Duke University advised by Dr. Claudia K. Gunsch, with an expected graduation of Spring/Summer 2027. I have over five years of research experience in microbiome engineering, mycology, microbial ecology, and environmental biotechnology. My research focuses on fungal microbiome engineering, with two primary projects: encapsulating microbes to enhance degradation of PAHs and other persistent contaminants as part of Duke's Superfund Research Center, and characterizing fungal communities that persist on solar panels. I have also led and collaborated on interdisciplinary projects with academic and industry partners including Solar Unsoiled, Duke’s General Robotics Lab, and Durham's local firefighter department. Our work is published in ASM Microbiology Resource Announcements and Frontiers in Microbiology, with additional manuscripts under review at the Journal of Hazardous Materials Advances, the Journal of Occupational and Environmental Hygiene, and Nature Communications. Beyond the laboratory, I am increasingly interested in the intersection of environmental science and litigation. I currently work part time in Duke’s Environmental Law and Policy Clinic as an environmental consultant, researching water quality, hazardous waste, and contaminated site remediation standards to inform legal strategy and agency comment letters. After graduating, I'm looking for research scientist roles in bioremediation, starting summer 2027. Outside the lab, I love to play soccer, practice my pottery skills, and read! Right now I'm also knitting my first sweater.
